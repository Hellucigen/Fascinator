Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"From Embodied Experience to Reusable Relational Knowledge: An Auditable Unified-Graph Architecture and a Preregistered Boundary Analysis of Activation-Based Access,"** for consideration in [JOURNAL NAME].

**Research problem.** Long-lived embodied agents accumulate experience far faster than they consolidate it: action histories remain logs instead of becoming reusable knowledge. Our manuscript addresses when experience written back as relational structure on a unified knowledge graph changes future behavior, and specifically when the graph's access mechanism — spreading activation — adds value over flat retrieval.

**Contributions.** (1) Fascinator (FAS), an auditable cognitive-architecture prototype in which semantic, episodic, and procedural content share one typed, weighted graph and a single evidence-weighted write-back channel converts experience into relational knowledge; (2) a preregistered evaluation protocol whose primary hypothesis — that spreading activation beats flat retrieval — was rejected and is reported as a primary result; and (3) a boundary analysis that maps exactly where activation-based access does help: multi-input convergence through a shared hub under weak edge weights and shallow paths (an 84-cell parameter sweep), plus a structural diagnosis (relation-direction semantics) of why propagation fails elsewhere.

**Negative results and preregistration.** The preregistered campaign (4 topologies × 50 paired seeds, 34,016 archived runs) rejected the incremental value of spreading activation over a closed-form flat baseline (ΔJCG < 0 under all four conditions, Holm p = 1.5×10⁻⁹, 0/200 reversals), and an activation-free embedding-retrieval control fully reproduces the paper's main positive behavioral result (beacon task 20/20 vs 0/20). We report these negative results at full strength, alongside two self-initiated assembly audits (one of which invalidated our own earlier routing campaign), because we consider the boundary of a mechanism as informative as its existence.

**Data and code availability.** All experiments are deterministic and fully archived: raw run records, graph snapshots, statistics files, and preregistration records ship with the submission. Zero-LLM experiments are bit-reproducible; a one-click reproduction script covers the headline numbers. We will deposit the complete archive (dataset) and code repository publicly upon acceptance, and are happy to provide reviewer access immediately.
